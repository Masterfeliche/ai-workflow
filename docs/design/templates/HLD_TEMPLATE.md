# High-Level Design (HLD)

- **Version:**

- **Owner / Reviewers:**
- **Date:**

## 1. Problem and objectives

Describe the business problem, users, desired outcomes and measurable success criteria.

## 2. Scope and constraints

In scope, out of scope, assumptions, regulatory/business constraints, existing-system constraints
and operational constraints.

## 3. Actors and system context

Identify human actors, external systems, trust boundaries and system responsibilities.

## 4. Domain model

Define core concepts, relationships, invariants, ownership and terminology.

## 5. Architecture overview

Describe the chosen architecture style and why it fits. Include a Mermaid context/container diagram.
Label synchronous, asynchronous and polling interactions.

## 6. Major components and responsibilities

| Component | Responsibility | Owns data | Interfaces | Failure boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 7. Key data flows and workflows

Include sequence diagrams for important happy paths and critical failure paths.

## 8. Data architecture

Describe storage choices, ownership, consistency, lifecycle, migrations and retention.

## 9. Security and privacy

Identify assets, trust boundaries, threat scenarios, authorization model, sensitive data and controls.

## 10. NFRs and capacity assumptions

State measurable latency, throughput, availability, durability, recovery, accessibility and cost goals.
Distinguish measured evidence from estimates and assumptions.

## 11. Deployment and operations

Describe environments, deployment units, configuration, secrets, observability, backup and recovery.

## 12. Alternatives and trade-offs

Compare viable options against explicit project criteria. Record consequential decisions as ADRs.

## 13. Risks, unknowns and discovery spikes

| Unknown / risk | Impact if false | Verification method | Owner / status |
|---|---|---|---|

## 14. Approval and traceability

List requirement IDs, EDDs, ADRs, feature SSOTs, review outcome and approval evidence.
